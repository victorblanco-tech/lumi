# Lumi 0.6.4-dev-34 — receive-gap timing repair

## Evidence before the repair

The owner left Player 1 running a physical CDJ loop. Dev-33 recorded a 1.75 s
gap in CDJ status/beat reception followed by advancing late status packets and
an out-of-order bar-beat packet. Bridge/local queue residence was small; the
recording does not determine whether the earlier delay arose in the player,
network, OS receive path or Java callback delivery.

The canonical position rewound from 184 to 183. A subsequent observation
created a new transport epoch and repeated the current AutoLoop. The next cue
was forecast four beats ahead, but held its first deadline despite newer
beats arriving sooner at unchanged BPM. Its dispatch was about 884 ms after
the reduced phrase boundary, although the MIDI worker missed its own deadline
by only 5 ms. Small MIDI-worker lateness alone was therefore not acceptance.

Private recordings are under `build/dev33-hardware/` locally, not public assets.
The anonymous receive sequence is included as an executable regression test.

## Changes

- Advancing status that trails a wall-clock prediction is no longer classified
  as a seek. Backward jumps and forward jumps ahead of elapsed time still use
  the established discontinuity checks.
- A reordered beat cannot rewind canonical transport without a confirmed
  discontinuity, nor refresh its timing anchor.
- New beats may retime an **unsent** cue at unchanged BPM. At its exact boundary,
  an unsent cue's deadline is reconciled with the signed offset. Retiming occurs
  atomically inside the MIDI lane, preserving generation and completion state.
  If the pulse has already been sent, retiming is a no-op: it cannot reinsert a
  command, retrigger the look, or steer SoundSwitch's running timeline.
- Diagnostics distinguish a confirmed seek from normal position advancement
  and record retiming requests. `scripts/analyze-live-timing.py` reads the file
  without owning a control socket or affecting app connectivity.

## Acceptance boundaries

This is not a SoundSwitch beat-feedback or phase-correction feature. The
hardware loop spans roughly beats 60–316. BD Start at 401 and BD Chorus at 432
are outside that loop and cannot be accepted from this test. Physical lighting
phase and a stopped-CDJ start also remain separate acceptance cases.

Automated and live acceptance results are recorded below when completed.

- Engine library: 164 passed, 4 ignored. The initial sandbox-only run denied
  three local IPC socket tests; rerunning with the permitted socket access
  passed all 164.
- Pro DJ Link: 20 unit, 8 protocol, 20 provider and 1 supervisor tests passed,
  including the real receive-gap replay that failed on Dev-33.
- MIDI: 20 tests passed. The new test advances a pending deadline and then
  submits 20 post-send retime requests; exactly the original two pulses
  (Bank + AutoLoop) remain, with no reinsertion.
- Clippy (`-D warnings`), architecture and independent product-version checks
  passed. macOS package built and signature verified locally.
- Dev-33 UI: a future Theme change saved revision 2 without losing either
  loaded track. Both controlled app quits stopped all owned services. This
  is not yet Dev-34 timing acceptance.

### Dev-34 real hardware follow-up (10 October)

After the owner handled the macOS permission and the same installed app was
restarted, CHRM identity resolved. Both prepared tracks returned: Player 1
local CHRM and Player 2 CHRM via Player 1/LINK. Controlled shutdown was checked
for absence of the owned engine/Link processes before restart. No system
permission settings were changed by the test operator.

Two Arm/Start sequences were exercised through the desktop UI while Player 1
continued its existing loop. The second sequence armed beyond the last phrase
boundary inside the loop; the launch target correctly moved to beat 64 after
the natural loop return. This does not substitute for a stopped-player test.

Private captures in `build/dev34-hardware/` retained fourteen unique AutoLoop
dispatches (generations 2–15), each sent once and successful. Worker deadline
lateness was at most 5.033 ms in these captures. Matched phrase-boundary
observations showed sends approximately 45 ms before through effectively at
the reduced boundary, rather than the previous 884 ms after it. These are
internal reception/dispatch measurements, NOT physical SoundSwitch beat phase.

The two recorded seeks were the natural loop returns to beat 61; no extra
backward seek was observed in that interval. Receive queue p95 remained around
5 ms, but a 148 ms local queue outlier occurred during Arm, away from a cue
boundary. Its cause remains unproven and this run is not a jitter-free claim.

A future Bridge at beat 176 was changed from BLUE RED GREEN to GREEN PINK
through the live editor, with a missing-mapping hold later in the edited suffix.
Revision 2 saved without the fallback-edit error. SoundSwitch visibly selected
bank 2 and BRIDGE GREEN PINK, then the subsequent BD BRIDGE GREEN PINK command
was recorded. Both loaded tracks and plans remained available.

Still pending: BD Start/BD Chorus outside this physical loop, stopped-CDJ
starts, physical light phase, and longer-run jitter characterization. The app
remains a development candidate, not a fully accepted production release.

### Extended BD / buildup hardware loop — acceptance FAILED

The owner subsequently extended playback to include BD Start, BD Chorus and
short buildup phrases. Private evidence: `build/dev34-bd-loop/01.json` through
`03.json`. No CDJ controls, output offset or app configuration were changed
during this follow-up.

BD Start (phrase 9, beat 401) and BD Chorus (phrase 10, beat 432) were forecast
four beats / approximately 1548 ms ahead and dispatched successfully. Some
passes were close to the received/reduced boundary; other passes had gaps of
over two seconds in status/beat reception, despite small measured bridge and
engine queue residence. Precise-position messages continued around at least
some of those gaps. The source of those status/beat gaps is not established.

Critically, the short buildup sequence reproduced duplicate logical cue sends
within the SAME load, revision and transport epoch (75), with no intervening
seek:

- Phrase 12, bank 2 / AutoLoop 20: generation 75 dispatched at engine time
  approximately 791347798 us, then generation 77 at 791709537 us (362 ms later).
- Phrase 13, bank 2 / AutoLoop 22: generation 78 dispatched at approximately
  792902440 us, then generation 80 at 793729373 us (827 ms later).

In both cases the next future cue was scheduled immediately before the delayed
current-phrase observation caused a reactive repeat of the already-sent cue.
Generation-level deduplication is insufficient here: each repeat has a NEW
generation. The required follow-up is an executable replay of this ordering
and logical cue completion retention across scheduling the next future cue.
It must preserve deliberate seek/loop re-entry semantics, without adding
SoundSwitch phase correction or suppressing a genuinely new phrase.

This supersedes any inference that the earlier fourteen-send sample proved
absence of duplicate output generally. Exact physical beat phase is still not
measured. Do not promote this candidate based on MIDI deadline lateness alone.
