# Dev-37 — receive provenance and confirmed loop landing

Not production acceptance and not a claim that SoundSwitch beat phase is fixed.

## Evidence

A bounded real-Player packet capture showed monotonically increasing precise
positions at the interface, while corresponding bridge observations were
reordered. Existing publisher and engine queue diagnostics did not include
the earlier receive path. Dev-36 was an intermediate local measurement build:
it adds packet source IP and Beat Link DeviceUpdate construction time to the
bounded recorder. These are diagnostic fields, not new timing authorities.
Raw captures and user telemetry remain private and outside the repository.

The subsequent real-hardware measurement also exposed an independent, fully
reproducible provider defect. A 256-beat backward loop was confirmed by three
status frames, but the next beat packet was missing. Two beats of normal
status progression discarded the confirmed landing before the next bar beat.
The provider continued on beat 616 instead of 360. The anonymized exact event
sequence is now a regression test, observed failing before the fix.

## Change

Follow normal status progress of a confirmed landing until the next exact
beat, without extending its original one-second expiry. Backward/reordered
status, an inconsistent forward jump, or expired evidence revokes it. No
automatic SoundSwitch correction, retrigger policy, offset, waveform, USB
identity, or Link phase policy is changed.

## Remaining investigation

The second simultaneous capture (Dev-36, 14:40:36–14:41:05 local time) contains
964 monotone precise positions from Player 1. Of 853 uniquely matched values,
57 backwards transitions already exist in DeviceUpdate construction order.
All matched observations have the expected Player 1 source address. Relative
capture-to-construction delay spread reaches 7.362 seconds; subsequent
construction-to-publication time is at most 0.054 ms. This is a cross-clock
relative spread, not an independently calibrated absolute one-way delay.

The remaining problem is earlier than the measured callback/publication path.
Do not assert a specific Wi-Fi, macOS, or Beat Link root cause. A thread sample
showed the single BeatFinder receiver inside socket receive, not blocked on a
Lumi lock; that point-in-time observation is not exhaustive proof. Ordered
interface arrival is not proof of zero jitter. MIDI provider-call timing is
not physical beat sync. The loop fix does NOT claim to remove this early
receive delay.

## Desktop/hardware checkpoint

Dev-37 was installed, signature verified, and opened through the desktop UI.
The real Player 1 loop produced one accepted landing at zero-based beat 359;
later status beat 381 corresponded to reduced beat 380, rather than retaining
the previous 256-beat offset. This validates a real loop landing only, not the
complete AutoLoop output chain. That chain remains blocked: the UI reports
`USB identity reader: No route to host`, with external-track placeholders.
Dev-37 is left open in Live, Off; no physical light acceptance is claimed.
The user was asked to handle normal network permission prompts and whether a
wired comparison is possible. No OS network/security settings were changed.

## Initial Ethernet comparison

The user connected Ethernet and disabled Wi-Fi. After a controlled Dev-37
restart, the bridge bound to Ethernet, both Players and CHRM identity were
recognized, and the desktop showed the 18- and 33-phrase plans. Arm/Start was
exercised through the UI at zero offset. SoundSwitch visibly showed AutoLoop
playback and one Link peer at 155 BPM.

The first 54-second observation contained 1,799 precise Player 1 positions
without a backwards transition. At 90 seconds, 3,000 positions included the
expected physical loop wrap and three other backwards steps (30, 329 and
29 ms). Ethernet is therefore not proven free of receive reordering. Six
recorded AutoLoop sends succeeded, with worker deadline lateness between
0.007 and 5.039 ms. These are internal measurements, not physical phase proof.
The confirmed loop landed on the new timeline and continued progressing.

A matching bounded, passive Ethernet packet capture was requested from the
user to distinguish interface arrival from construction-time ordering. Its
result is pending; do not conclude that Wi-Fi alone caused the earlier
multi-second delay or declare complete timing acceptance from this checkpoint.

## Completed Ethernet packet comparison

The user's successful Ethernet capture covers 14:59:24.771800 through
14:59:53.781525 local time on 2026-10-10. It contains 968 precise-position,
183 status, 75 beat and 14 announcement packets. Captured precise positions
are monotone. Match only positions occurring exactly once in both the capture
and retained Player 1 diagnostic window: 935 samples meet this condition.
Ordering these by DeviceUpdate construction time exposes 30 backwards
transitions, despite the ordered capture. All origins match Player 1.

Relative capture-to-construction delay spread is 10.443 ms median,
1,632.634 ms p95 and 2,542.538 ms maximum (anchored to the fastest matched
sample, not calibrated absolute latency). This disproves Wi-Fi as a necessary
condition for the observed failure. It does not identify Java, Beat Link,
socket delivery or OS filtering as the cause. No timing-policy workaround or
security/network setting has been changed in response.

An additional observation for investigation: 968 captured precise packets
use 952 distinct UDP source ports; the previous Wi-Fi capture similarly uses
948 ports across 964 precise packets. This is not evidence of a defect by
itself. Any receive-path comparison must retain this traffic characteristic,
rather than testing only one steady UDP flow. Raw packet captures remain
private under ignored build directories.
